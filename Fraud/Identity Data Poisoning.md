When bad actors deliberately inject fake identity data into credit bureau databases and identity verification networks like LexisNexis and Socure, the correct industry terms are **"Synthetic Identity Fraud Profile Seeding"** or **"Credit Profile Establishment"**. \[<a href="https://www.socure.com/glossary/synthetic-identity-fraud" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>1</u></a>, <a href="https://www.innovatrics.com/glossary/synthetic-identity-fraud/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>2</u></a>, <a href="https://www.synthetic-identity-fraud.com/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>3</u></a>\]
The overall mechanism is known as **Synthetic Identity Fraud (SIF)**. Within that framework, the exact tactical terms used by fraud analysts and risk providers describe the specific stages of injecting that data: \[<a href="https://www.socure.com/glossary/synthetic-identity-fraud" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>1</u></a>, <a href="https://www.synthetic-identity-fraud.com/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>2</u></a>\]

## 1. Profile Seeding (or Creation)
This is the foundational step of injecting fake data. Fraudsters combine a real piece of stolen data (like a child's stolen Social Security Number) with fake names and addresses. They apply for a product they know they will get rejected for (like a store credit card). Because the credit bureau cannot find a match, it automatically creates a brand-new, empty credit file for that fake entity. \[<a href="https://www.socure.com/glossary/synthetic-identity" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>1</u></a>, <a href="https://www.synthetic-identity-fraud.com/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>2</u></a>, <a href="https://www.transunion.com/blog/what-is-synthetic-identity-fraud" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>3</u></a>, <a href="https://www.innovatrics.com/glossary/synthetic-identity-fraud/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>4</u></a>, <a href="https://integratedbiometrics.com/press-releases/synthetic-identity-fraud" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>5</u></a>\]

## 2. Credit File Poisoning (or Database Pollution)
From the perspective of data integrity, this specific effect is called database pollution. When a credit bureau or data aggregator creates a record for a fake entity, the underlying ecosystem becomes "polluted" or "poisoned". This forces identity verification platforms to validate future queries against fraudulent reference data, thinking the person is real. \[<a href="https://www.socure.com/glossary/synthetic-identity" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>1</u></a>, <a href="https://www.experian.co.uk/blogs/latest-thinking/guide/identity-verification-what-is-it-and-why-is-it-important/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>2</u></a>, <a href="https://searchinform.com/articles/cybersecurity/cyber-threats/fraud/type/synthetic-identity-fraud/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>3</u></a>, <a href="https://www.unit21.ai/fraud-aml-dictionary/identity-verification" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>4</u></a>\]

## 3. Identity Seasoning
Once the profile is seeded into the database, fraudsters engage in "identity seasoning" or "piggybacking". They add the fake identity as an authorized user on legitimate credit card accounts. This tricks the credit bureau into generating a high credit score for the fictional person before the final "bust-out" phase. \[<a href="https://www.transunion.com/blog/what-is-synthetic-identity-fraud" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>1</u></a>, <a href="https://www.innovatrics.com/glossary/synthetic-identity-fraud/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>2</u></a>, <a href="https://regulaforensics.com/blog/synthetic-identity-fraud/" rel="noopener" class="external-link" target="_blank"><u>3</u></a>\]
